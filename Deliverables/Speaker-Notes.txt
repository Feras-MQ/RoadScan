SLIDE 1
MAIN TALK — target 25 seconds. Rehearsal timing remains unmeasured.

A pothole can be obvious. Smaller cracks can be much harder to notice in a road image. Taking a photo is a useful start, but someone still needs to look closely, identify what needs attention, and record the decision. That is the starting point for RoadScan.

SLIDE 2
MAIN TALK — target 35 seconds. Rehearsal timing remains unmeasured.

Meet RoadScan. You upload a road photo. A trained detector suggests where damage may be. Then a person checks the result, corrects it, and saves the review. These three steps are the core of the project: upload, detect and review. AI helps point out what to inspect; the person keeps the final decision.

SLIDE 3
MAIN TALK — target 50 seconds. Rehearsal timing remains unmeasured.

These are saved predictions from our Main YOLO26s model on two road images in the project. The boxes show where it suggested cracks or potholes. We have kept every box from the supplied scorecard, including the dashed boxes it flagged as false alarms. The examples also contain missed damage. That makes the model's role clear: it gives the reviewer a starting point, rather than a final verdict. These selected examples show the kind of output the model produces. They are not a new accuracy test. Next, we will explain how the Main model and the road variants developed.

SLIDE 4
MAIN TALK — target 45 seconds. Rehearsal timing remains unmeasured.

We explored more than one detector family. The project includes YOLO11 nano, small and medium; YOLOv8 medium; and YOLO26 nano and small. We also explored RT-DETR L and X, which use a transformer-based detector design. ResNet18 is a separate image-level baseline: it predicts which damage types are present but does not locate them. The current inventory records 19 detector choices across models and training variants. That is an inventory count, not 19 fair head-to-head tests. The model engineering story here focuses on YOLO26s Main and its later Road fine-tunes.

SLIDE 5
MAIN TALK — target 65 seconds. Rehearsal timing remains unmeasured.

We started with pretrained YOLO detectors and adapted them to road damage. The recorded RDD training split contains about 27,000 images, with separate validation and test groups of 5,758 each. The work included checking the split and labels, then experimenting with model size, resolution, augmentation and label cleaning. The 640-pixel experiments favored small over nano and standard augmentation over no augmentation. Label cleaning did not improve the validation measure, so Main kept the original labels. Main used pretrained YOLO26s at 1024 pixels, with mosaic and horizontal flips rather than vertical flips. Its recorded run lasted 120 epochs and selected epoch 84 on validation. Later, Road v1, A and v2b explored further adaptation to reviewed road frames.

SLIDE 6
MAIN TALK — target 60 seconds. Rehearsal timing remains unmeasured.

Main's reported Ultralytics RDD test result is 63.04 percent mAP50 and 33.01 percent at the stricter mAP50–95 measure. These measure box detection across four damage classes, not the percentage of photos that are correct. The smaller experiments helped us understand the choices: at 640 pixels, small scored above nano, and standard augmentation scored above no augmentation. Main also used 1024-pixel input and other training choices; its exact external-data exposure is unresolved, so we cannot attribute its result to one change. The source audit found near-duplicate leakage, and these historical scores have not been independently rerun. That is why predictions still need review.

SLIDE 7
MAIN TALK — target 55 seconds. Rehearsal timing remains unmeasured.

Detection is useful only if the result can be checked. The reviewer looks at the whole image, removes a wrong box, changes a label or adds something the model missed. These are the actual decision controls. In this example, the pothole annotation was manual and no inference was run. The screenshot shows the editing stage, with unsaved changes. The note was saved afterward in the demonstration workspace. A reviewer can also compare model opinions and choose an individual result before saving. Agreement does not guarantee correctness. The point is to make the human review explicit, instead of treating the first set of boxes as the final answer.

SLIDE 8
MAIN TALK — 85 seconds explanation + 150 seconds live demonstration; target 235 seconds total. Rehearsal timing remains unmeasured.

The full system goes beyond drawing boxes. A selected detector returns damage classes, locations and confidence. A documented formula combines type, relative box area, count and confidence into a priority score. That score helps order a review queue; it is not measured pavement severity. The optional duplicate branch crops damage, makes DINOv2 embeddings and searches nearby open reports with FAISS. It offers candidates, never an automatic merge. The implementation is present, but the optional FAISS path was not accepted end to end in the latest checks and its threshold remains uncalibrated. We must say that plainly rather than imply every branch is proven.

LIVE DEMO CUE — reserve 2.5 minutes; leave the slides and use the actual app.
Ask the panel to choose an unseen road image. Confirm the intended model is active before the demonstration. Upload the image, show actual boxes/classes/confidence, then explain the priority components and review the result. Save only after the reviewer confirms. Show its queue position. If a second view and the optional duplicate dependencies are available, inspect the actual candidate list and similarity score; otherwise state the unavailable branch rather than substituting a prepared screenshot. Missing or simulated coordinates must be labeled. Do not call a stored screenshot a live demo, do not claim an unseen-input pass before it happens, and do not change the finished videos. Budget: first image and detection55sec, priority/review/save45sec, second view/duplicate status and queue50sec. If inference fails, show the real error and clearly identify any recorded backup as a fallback.

SLIDE 9
MAIN TALK — target 30 seconds. Rehearsal timing remains unmeasured.

RoadScan connects damage detection with a decision a person can inspect and correct. Main's reported RDD results are promising, but we still need a protected local road test and real duplicate-pair calibration. The next step is to collect and label that evidence, test without tuning on it, and use the failures to guide further development.

SLIDE 10
BACKUP — outside the ten-minute presentation and live demo.

Road fine-tunes use the same 343-frame selection set: Road v1 46.74/31.26%; Road A 46.80/28.98%; Road v2b 48.61/29.37% (mAP50/mAP50–95). Main 63.04/33.01% is a separate team-reported RDD protocol. These are not a shared independent ranking.

SLIDE 11
BACKUP — optional map and curated guide; outside the ten-minute presentation and live demo.

After the review, the report should be easy to find again. Search and filters help locate it, and the coordinate view offers another route when a location has been supplied. These are example coordinates entered manually in an isolated demonstration, not verified field GPS. The map links back to the report and its review; it does not verify GPS. The help panel explains the steps in English or Arabic and can point the user to the right screen. Its instructions are written in advance, not generated by an AI assistant. Together, the report list, coordinate view and guide help a user return to the evidence without pretending that a pin is proof.
