SLIDE 1 / 20 seconds
Road damage needs a reviewable record before it can support a maintenance decision. RoadScan connects local detection to human correction, priority support and controlled preparation of training examples. Our contribution is that complete inspection workflow. This remains a capstone prototype, rather than structural safety certification.
Sources: RoadScan-Project-Report.pdf sections 1–2; OLO AIEP brief, Track A; assets/readme/overview.png, original RoadScene illustration. Team members supplied by the project owner.

SLIDE 2 / 50 seconds
The browser accepts photos, a camera or video. App.tsx and LiveRecognition.tsx call FastAPI in api.py, which checks access, input size and image dimensions. ModelRuntime in model_library.py selects the checkpoint, while inference.py runs local detection. There are two paths. Temporary recognition returns boxes without saving a report. Explicit report creation stores the image, initial prediction and a reviewable record. Review and workflow modules validate corrections and status changes, retaining audit history in SQLite. Approved examples can enter a training export. Keeping these boundaries separate avoids turning every live camera frame into permanent project data.
Sources: roadscan/api.py:136,151,280; model_library.py:220; inference.py:78; core.py; review.py; workflow.py; training_data.py; frontend/src/App.tsx and LiveRecognition.tsx. Diagram is native editable slide objects.

SLIDE 3 / 45 seconds
This is the real review interface with private road imagery excluded. A reviewer checks the detection boxes, corrects them and records a decision. Model comparison runs two or three checkpoints sequentially and keeps their individual outputs visible. A majority is one suggestion. If the third model is more credible, the reviewer can choose that model instead. The choice only replaces draft boxes until the review is explicitly saved. Priority provides a transparent triage aid. It cannot infer physical crack depth or certify whether a road is safe from one photograph.
Sources: assets/readme/review.png, cleared screenshot; frontend/src/ModelVote.tsx:45 and App.tsx:337; roadscan/voting.py; vote_worker.py; review.py; priority.py. Screenshot shows the application controls, not an accuracy evaluation.

SLIDE 5 / 45 seconds
For a laptop, USB camera or Iriun phone camera, live recognition analyzes fresh frames automatically and shows an overlay. It samples at the pace the detector can sustain. For an uploaded file, Analyze every frame is different. The video module decodes frames sequentially and waits for inference before continuing. A busy response retains the same frame for retry, and completion checks available metadata. This improves decoded-frame coverage, but it can take longer than the video’s playback duration. Heavy models such as RT-DETR may be unsuitable for responsive CPU viewing. Saving a selected frame remains an explicit action.
Sources: frontend/src/LiveRecognition.tsx; liveFrames.ts; FullVideoAnalysis.tsx:34; roadscan/video_analysis.py:93; api.py video-analysis routes. Hardware-specific sustained frame rate and drone integration remain unverified.

SLIDE 6 / 40 seconds
A saved review does not teach the active model instantly. First, the reviewer corrects the boxes, confirms the report and approves the example. The Learning screen exports approved images, labels, split information and provenance. A trainer prepares that export for a separate training run, evaluates the new checkpoint on unused test data and imports it through the model library. The current export needs dataset-layout preparation for the training runner, so we do not claim one-click retraining. This deliberate boundary keeps an incorrect review from silently changing a model during a demonstration.
Sources: assets/readme/learning.png; TrainingApproval.tsx; Learning.tsx; roadscan/training_data.py:40,78; detector_train.py:71; ModelLibrary.tsx:49. Export images/train layout requires preparation for the runner’s train/images expectation.

SLIDE 7 / 45 seconds
The recorded release checks passed two hundred twenty-one backend tests, twenty-four frontend tests and sixty-seven browser checks. One optional backend test was skipped. These establish software behavior, not detection accuracy. Our delivery includes source code, reports, guides and a published baseline checkpoint with its scoped source license. The remaining research work is concrete: collect labeled Saudi road images and real repeated-damage pairs, repair data leakage, compare models on the same test and measure camera latency on representative hardware. RoadScan provides a working, inspectable foundation for that work, with human review central to the workflow.
